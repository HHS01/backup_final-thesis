What Is This & How to Use It

This reference card helps any teacher plan a 3-station rotational lesson when the classroom has fewer devices than students — the most common situation in Sub-Saharan African schools (median ratio: 1 device per 13–18 pupils).

The 3-station model guarantees every student reaches the digital station within a single lesson, without chaos or idle time. Use this card to:

  1. Understand the station roles (below)
  2. Look up pre-calculated cohort splits for your class size (Reference Tables)
  3. Fill in your own lesson timetable (Fill-In Planner section)
The Three Stations
💻 Station 1: Digital Lab

Students use the available tablets/computers. With Paired Learning (2 per screen), more students are covered per device. Cohort rotates every cycle.

📖 Station 2: Collaborative

Students solve syllabus workbook problems or structured peer activities in small groups. Teacher-independent — self-guided tasks.

🎓 Station 3: Teacher Focus

Direct teacher instruction, literacy remediation, phonics drilling, or targeted support for the cohort that needs it most in this cycle.

Quick-Reference: Cohort Splits by Class Size

Cohorts are balanced using: Cohort A = ⌈N÷3⌉, Cohort B = round((N−A)÷2), Cohort C = N−A−B. The largest cohort always gets the Teacher Focus station first (highest need).

Research Basis

3-Station Rotation Model — Pedagogical literature on blended learning in device-scarce environments (Graham & Halverson, 2012; Raman, 2025) confirms that structured cohort rotation is the single most effective strategy for maximising digital access equity when hardware-to-pupil ratios exceed 1:5.

SSA Class Size Context — UIS/UNESCO (2024) data shows median lower-secondary class sizes of 42–58 pupils in Kenya, Rwanda, and Zambia, with device:pupil ratios ranging from 1:8 (Rwanda national average) to 1:28 (rural Sub-Saharan Africa). The 3-station model operates effectively across the full 15–90 pupil range covered by this planner.

Zero Idle Time Principle — The 5-minute whole-class priming and synthesis buffer prevents cohort transition dead-time. Research shows unstructured transition periods account for 12–18% of lost instructional time in rotation models without explicit protocols (Pouye, 2024).

Practical Tips for SSA Classrooms
Challenge Strategy
Power cuts mid-lesson Use Kolibri or Kiwix (see DPG Directory) — fully offline on a single charged laptop. Pre-download content the day before.
Students unfamiliar with devices Assign the most tech-confident pupils as "Digital Station Leads" for the first 2 weeks. Rotate the leadership role.
No clock / watch in classroom Use door-knock signals or a student timekeeper with a watch to signal rotation transitions at pre-set intervals.
High deficit (fewer than 5 seats for 17 pupils) Form 3-student trios: one operates the device, one records results on paper, one asks questions. All switch roles in the next lesson.
Mixed-ability class Assign higher-ability pupils to Collaborative station first (self-guided tasks) so Teacher Focus station can target lower-ability cohort in Rotation 1 while you have energy and focus.
